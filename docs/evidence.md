# Evidence for Penny

*Sources behind the pitch and the one-page summary, gathered 8 Nov. Each claim has its strength, how to say it,
and what not to say. Open every source before quoting it on a slide: these notes come from search summaries.*

The judges score **relevance and impact (20%)** on "a well-evidenced need". The strongest story uses evidence
that is UK-specific, recent and modest in its claims.

## 1. How many people this is for

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Hundreds of thousands of older people live in care homes | **278,946 people aged 65+** lived in care homes in England and Wales in 2021 (82% of all care home residents) | Strong: official census |

Source: ONS, [Older people living in care homes in 2021 and changes since 2011](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/ageing/articles/olderpeoplelivingincarehomesin2021andchangessince2011/2023-10-09).

**Say:** "Nearly 280,000 people over 65 live in care homes in England and Wales."

## 2. Long afternoons with nothing to do (our core problem)

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Residents spend much of the day inactive | Studies mapping residents' days find **around half of waking time is spent inactive or dozing** | Moderate: several observation studies, mostly 2000s–2010s |
| Little of the day is spent with other people | In a six-hour daytime observation in dementia care, residents spent **about 14% of the time (50 minutes) communicating** with staff or other residents | Moderate: one well-known UK study (BMJ); check the original before quoting |
| Staff are too busy for activities | A CQC inspection found staff had no time for **"conversation or meaningful activity"** because they were busy with care tasks; Healthwatch reviews of 31 homes recommended activity plans for every resident | Illustrative: individual reports, not national data |

Sources: University of Kent, "Being occupied: supporting meaningful activity in care homes for older people in
England", *Ageing & Society* ([Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/30EF6B3BAA03086D22B430B6C7867D9E/core-reader));
[Cardiff ORCA 18405](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/18405) (single-home observation study);
the 14% figure as cited in an [NIHR programme report](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/ukpgfar0909/app1);
CQC story via [carehome.co.uk](https://carehome.co.uk/news/article.cfm/id/1660559/dewsbury-care-home-bored);
[Healthwatch "Living life to the full"](https://nds.healthwatch.co.uk/node/1251).

**Say:** "Research in English care homes found residents spend around half their waking time inactive or dozing."
**Don't say:** "residents are bored all day" or any figure without its source on the slide.

## 3. Nobody free to play: the staffing gap

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Care is short-staffed | Skills for Care: **131,000 vacancies (8.3%)** in adult social care in 2023/24, falling to **111,000 (7.0%)** in 2024/25 | Strong: the sector's official workforce data |

Sources: [Personnel Today](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/calls-for-urgent-action-to-tackle-adult-social-care-recruitment-crisis/),
[Express & Star](https://www.expressandstar.com/uk-news/2025/07/28/adult-social-care-vacancies-down-but-domestic-recruitment-still-challenging),
[HFMA](https://www.hfma.org.uk/news-alerts/vacancies-down-adult-social-care). Quote the latest Skills for Care report directly.

**Say:** "On a typical day there are over 100,000 unfilled posts in adult social care in England. Priya doesn't
have a spare hour."
**Don't say:** that Penny replaces staff. She covers the hour they don't have.

## 4. Why paper, not a tablet

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Many older people don't use the internet | Age UK (2025): **2.4 million older people (19%)** use the internet less than once a month or not at all; 2024: **2.3 million aged 65+** don't use it | Strong: Age UK analysis of national data |
| Exclusion rises with age and living alone | Almost half of over-65s who never go online are over 75; **30%** of older people who live alone have limited use | Strong |

Sources: [Age UK internet use statistics, July 2025](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/siteassets/documents/reports-and-publications/reports-and-briefings/switched-off/internet-use-statistics-july-2025.pdf),
[Age UK press release](https://www.ageuk.org.uk/latest-press/articles/age-uk-warns-22.4-million-digitally-excluded-older-people-are-at-risk-of-being-left-behind-in-an-increasingly-digital-world/).

**Say:** "Nearly one in five older people barely use the internet. Penny works on paper, which everyone already knows."

## 5. Hands that won't hold a pen steadily

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Tremor is common in later life | Essential tremor affects about **4–6% of people over 65**, rising steeply with age (one pooled analysis: 74% more per decade) | Strong: meta-analyses |
| Stroke often leaves the arm and hand weak | About **40% of stroke survivors** have a lasting loss of arm function; about half with early arm problems still have them years later | Strong: Cochrane-based overview |

Sources: [BMJ review on essential tremor](https://www.bmj.com/content/366/bmj.l4485.full.pdf),
[PMC 3047762](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3047762), [PMC 2744469](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2744469);
upper limb after stroke: [ARNI summary](https://arni.uk.com/upper-limb-after-stroke-can-we-predict-recovery),
[NIHR](https://njl-admin.nihr.ac.uk/document/download/2047246).

**Say:** "Tremor, arthritis and stroke take away the pen long before they take away the enjoyment of the game."

## 6. Games are good for people (use carefully)

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| People who play board and card games tend to stay sharper | French Paquid cohort (BMJ Open, 2013): board game players had about **15% lower risk of dementia over 20 years**, partly linked to less cognitive decline and depression; "board games" included cards, draughts and bingo | Moderate: large cohort, but **observational** (association, not cause) |
| Short game programmes help cognition | A 2023 meta-analysis of trials found cognitive benefits from 3–6 month board game programmes in people over 60 | Moderate |

Sources: Dartigues et al., [BMJ Open 2013](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/3/8/e002998);
[Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2022.966647/text).

**Say:** "Playing games is linked with healthier ageing, and it's fun."
**Don't say:** "Penny prevents dementia" or "games reduce dementia risk by 15%". We pitch entertainment, not therapy.

## 7. Older people accept robots that play with them

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Residents come back to play with a robot, and talk to each other more | Vanderbilt pilot in two nursing homes: **14 of 15** residents with early dementia kept returning to a robot-guided game, playing longer each time; staff saw them engage more with other residents | Weak to moderate: small pilot, but directly on point |
| Acceptance depends on real benefit and practicality | A 2023 scoping review of **26 studies** of social robots in nursing homes: positive effects on residents helped acceptance; practical difficulties were the main barrier | Moderate |
| Not everyone wants a robot | A review of robot pets in care homes found engagement benefited residents' wellbeing, but **not everyone chose to engage** | Moderate |

Sources: [Vanderbilt Engineering](https://engineering.vanderbilt.edu/2019/03/18/robot-guided-video-game-prompts-peer-interaction-among-older-adults/);
[International Journal of Social Robotics 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s12369-023-01012-1);
robot pets: [Abbott et al. via PMC 6766882](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6766882).

**Say:** "In pilots, residents kept coming back to play with a robot, and started talking to each other more. That's
what we want Penny to do: give people something to share." This supports the "games that spread" beat.
**Use the honest point too:** Penny is optional. Residents choose to play.

## 8. Nostalgia and sharing memories (supporting, not central)

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Nostalgia builds social connection and meaning | Research by Sedikides, Wildschut and colleagues links nostalgia to feeling connected to others and finding life meaningful | Moderate, but **mostly from younger samples** |
| Older people's memories connect generations | When young adults read older adults' nostalgic stories, they felt more socially connected and found more meaning | Moderate: experimental |
| Reminiscence therapy itself | Cochrane review (2018, 22 trials, 1,972 people with dementia): effects on quality of life were **small and inconsistent** | Moderate evidence of **small** effects |

Sources: Wildschut, Sedikides & Robertson, *Memory* 2018 ([Southampton eprints](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/420141));
[Tilburg University](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/how-nostalgia-infuses-life-with-meaning-from-social-connectedness/);
[Cochrane CD001120](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD001120.pub3).

**Say:** "Sharing a game from your childhood is a way of sharing a memory."
**Don't say:** "Penny delivers reminiscence therapy". The clinical evidence is weak, and we don't need it.

## 9. Loneliness (context only)

| Claim | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Severe loneliness is common in care homes | Meta-analysis: about **35%** of care home residents severely lonely (95% CI 14–60%, very wide); earlier estimate 22–42% vs 10% in the community | Moderate, uncertain |

Sources: Gardiner et al., University of Sheffield ([White Rose 160982](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/160982/)).

Our story leads with entertainment and nostalgia, not loneliness. If it comes up in questions: "Loneliness is
common in care homes, and we don't claim a robot fixes it. Penny gives people something to do and something to share."

## 10. Figures checked from the team's notes

| Claim as written in the notes | Verdict | Use |
|---|---|---|
| "1 in 3 seniors experience social isolation" | **Corrected:** WHO says about **1 in 4** older people are socially isolated ([WHO](https://www.who.int/activities/reducing-social-isolation-and-loneliness-among-older-people)) | "1 in 4 older people are socially isolated" |
| "Social isolation increases mortality risk by up to 29%" | **Checks out:** Holt-Lunstad et al. (2015), 70 studies, 3.4 million people; it's 29% higher *odds* ([BYU](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/7516)) | "linked with 29% higher odds of early death" |
| "Adults over 50 using digital/gaming tech show less cognitive decline" | **Checks out, observational:** Benge and Scullin, *Nature Human Behaviour* (2025), 411,430 adults, lower odds of cognitive impairment (OR 0.42); about digital technology generally, not games | "linked with lower odds of cognitive impairment" |
| "Mentor/teacher roles reduce depression" | **Partly:** volunteering is linked with lower depression and longer life (Jenkinson et al., *BMC Public Health* 2013), mostly cohort studies ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3766013)) | "volunteering and mentoring are linked with…"; supports residents teaching Penny |

| "32–40% of older adults use games to fight boredom" | **No source found** | Don't use |
| "Mobile games lose over 80% of seniors after week one" | **No source found** | Don't use |
| "Over 40% of lonely retirees feel no longer needed" | **No source found** | Don't use |

Unsourced figures can go back in only with the original source attached.

## The five numbers for the slides

1. **~280,000** people over 65 live in care homes in England and Wales (ONS, 2021).
2. **About half** of residents' waking time is inactive or dozing (University of Kent, *Ageing & Society*).
3. **1 in 4** older people are socially isolated (WHO), linked with **29% higher odds of early death**
   (Holt-Lunstad et al., 2015).
4. **111,000** unfilled posts in adult social care in England, 2024/25 (Skills for Care).
5. **2.4 million** older people (19%) barely use the internet (Age UK, 2025).
6. **~40%** of stroke survivors have lasting arm problems; **4–6%** of over-65s have essential tremor.

## Before using any of these

- [ ] Open the original source and copy the exact figure and year.
- [ ] Put the source in small print on the slide.
- [ ] Use association language for the games studies ("linked with"), never "causes" or "prevents".
